## What is new

The Laplace transform as an improper integral taking f(t) to F(s), the time and frequency domains, Table 6.1 built from the definition, the unit step (Heaviside) function, linearity, exponential order with existence and uniqueness and the decay of F at infinity, the inverse transform computed by partial fractions and the first shifting property, proper rational functions (6.1). Transforms of derivatives (Table 6.2) and the method for linear constant-coefficient initial value problems, piecewise functions built from Heaviside steps, the second shifting property, the transfer function H(s) = X(s)/F(s), transforms of integrals and integral equations, transforms of periodic functions and the sawtooth (6.2). The convolution, its product-like properties, the convolution theorem, the solution of a forced oscillation with arbitrary forcing as a definite integral, resonance seen through the convolution, the Volterra integral equation (6.3). The rectangular pulse, the Dirac delta as a generalized function and as a limit of unit-mass pulses, its transform, the delta as the derivative of the Heaviside function, inverse transforms of improper rational functions, the impulse response and the claim that the output for any input is the convolution with it, the Euler–Bernoulli equation and three-point beam bending solved by a transform in x (6.4). The transform in t of a PDE in x and t, the transport equation on a half line with its wavefront, ill-posedness detected by a transform that does not decay, a bounded solution on a half-infinite rod, the heat equation with a flux condition inverted through Appendix B (6.5).

Calculus overlap: Calculus 7.7 defines the Laplace transform (`laplace-transform`, reused as this chapter's id for the same concept, declared here with type `transform`) and the improper integral with its comparison theorem (links from 6.1's existence argument); 7.1 integration by parts (Examples 6.1.3, 6.2's derivative rule); 7.4 partial fractions (`partial-fraction-decomposition`, reused as the id of 6.1's method, kind `skill` here); 4.8 L'Hôpital's rule (Exercise 6.1.3); 1.3 periodic functions (6.2). Calculus has no Heaviside, delta, convolution or transfer function. Ids of this book reused from earlier chapters: `transport-equation` and `convection` (1.9; the italic "convection equation" of 6.5 marks `transport-equation`), `method-of-characteristics`, `characteristic-curve` (1.9), `mass-spring-system`, `linear-operator`, `natural-frequency`, `resonance`, `rlc-series-circuit` (Chapter 2, from its plans), `heat-equation`, `wave-equation`, `piecewise-continuous-function` and `piecewise-smooth-function` (Chapter 4, from `ch04/book-rows.json`; 4.3 defines the piecewise terms that 6.1 and 6.2 recall), `hinged-end-of-a-beam` (Chapter 5, 5.2; the simple supports of 6.4). ## What would make this chapter stunning (root rule 23)

The chapter's one picture is a pair: a function of time on the left and its transform on the right, and every rule of the chapter is a move on one side that is a simpler move on the other. The pages can draw the pair live. In 6.1 the integrand e^(−st) f(t) is shaded under a slider for s, its area is the number F(s), and as s slides the point traces the transform's graph beside it; pull s down toward the growth rate of f and the area runs away, which is "exists for s > c" seen rather than stated. In 6.2 the Heaviside step is a switch the reader slides along t, a window and a signal switched on at π are its choices, and the rocket of Example 6.2.2 fires for a while the reader sets: the cart swings, and the firing time 2π on the slider is the one at which the mass stops dead afterwards (Exercise 6.2.10 b). In 6.3 the convolution is one function flipped and slid across the other, the overlap's area dropping onto the graph of f ∗ g as t runs, on the book's e^t ∗ t and sin ∗ cos, where the growing t sin ωt of resonance appears. In 6.4 a pulse narrows while its area stays 1 and its transform rises to e^(−as); the response to any input is built before the reader's eyes as a sum of impulse responses, one launched by each slice of the input, more slices converging on the convolution; and the beam sags under a load the reader moves along it. In 6.5 the toxic goo advances along the river at speed α while the wavefront line rises through the (x, t) plane beneath, and every characteristic of the ill-posed problem is seen to hit both axes where the data disagree.
