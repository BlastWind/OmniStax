## What would make this chapter stunning (root rule 23)

The chapter is one idea seen through three equations: a function is a sum of eigenfunctions, and a linear equation acts on each eigenfunction by a number. Every page can let the reader take the sum apart. In 4.1 the reader hunts for the λ at which x(π) returns to zero and watches the whirling string pop out only at those speeds. In 4.2 the Fourier coefficient is an area the reader can see cancel or add, and the partial sum closes on the sawtooth and the square wave as N climbs, the probe showing the midpoint at a jump and the Gibbs overshoot that never shrinks. In 4.3 the coefficients' decay is read off a log plot against the function's corners and jumps; in 4.4 the same function wears its odd and its even extension and the series that fits it better is the one with the faster decay; the boundary value problem's series blows up as λ nears an eigenvalue, the Fredholm alternative made visible. In 4.5 the jetpack cart runs on its steady periodic motion while the forcing's harmonics stand on a frequency axis beside ω₀, the one that lands on it lighting up and growing as t. In 4.6 the three 3Blue1Brown survivors are built: a rod of beads each drifting toward its neighbours' average (where u_xx comes from), the heat profile moving in time with its modes as bars dying off fastest first, the midpoint temperature falling to half at t ≈ 24.5. In 4.7 the plucked string moves on its guitar with its spectrum beside it; in 4.8 the two half-height waves run apart and reflect, their sum the string, and the (x, t) plane shows the two characteristics every point hears from. In 4.9 and 4.10 the plate and the disc are surfaces the reader turns, the boundary data a choice, and the Poisson kernel is a bump of weight that sharpens as the point nears the rim.
