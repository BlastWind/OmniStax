## What is new

Power series as the object (coefficients, center, partial sums, convergence and absolute convergence, divergence), the radius of convergence and Theorem 7.1.1, the ratio and root tests and their power-series form (Theorem 7.1.2) with the endpoint caveat, analytic functions and the Taylor series, term-by-term differentiation with the same radius, reindexing, the sum, constant multiple and product of series with their radii, polynomials as finite power series, the geometric series and the fact that a series defines its function only on an interval, expanding a rational function by the geometric series and partial fractions (7.1); ordinary and singular points of P y″ + Q y′ + R y = 0, the power-series ansatz, the recurrence relation with a₀ and a₁ arbitrary, the two solutions y₁ and y₂ with y₁(0) = 1, y₁′(0) = 0 and y₂(0) = 0, y₂′(0) = 1, Airy's equation and its two non-elementary solutions, Hermite's equation and its polynomial solutions (7.2); singular points where the power series finds only y = 0, the ansatz x^r, Frobenius-type solutions, the indicial equation and its double root, the second solution with a logarithm, regular singular points by two limits, Theorem 7.3.1 and the six-case method of Frobenius (distinct roots, a double root, roots differing by an integer, complex roots), Bessel's equation of order p, its Frobenius solutions, the gamma function, the Bessel functions of the first kind J_p and J_{−p}, J_{−n} = (−1)ⁿJ_n, the Bessel functions of the second kind Y_n as a limit, the general solution A J_n + B Y_n, and the drum equation xy″ + y′ + λ²xy = 0 solved by J₀(λx), Y₀(λx) (7.3).

## What would make this chapter stunning (root rule 23)

The chapter is one picture: a function built out of a growing pile of powers. Every page can let the reader watch the pile grow. In 7.1 the partial sums of a chosen series climb onto their function inside the interval of convergence and fly off outside it, even where the function is perfectly fine (the geometric series left of −1), the number line of Figure 7.1 underneath them with x₀ ± ρ marked; the Taylor approximations of sin x close in as the degree rises, the stretch where they stay within 0.1 of the sine bracketed on the axis and growing, and the centre x₀ slid along to show "near x₀" is where the approximation is good. In 7.2 the recurrence is a machine: a₀ and a₁ on sliders feed the coefficient bars two places at a time, the even bars from a₀ and the odd from a₁, and the partial sum above them settles on cosh x and sinh x; Airy's two solutions grow from their recurrences term by term, the series needing more and more terms to reach into the oscillating side; at an even Hermite order the even bars stop dead and y₁ is a polynomial while y₂ goes on forever. In 7.3 the singular point is a dot on the axis, x^r sweeps through its shapes as r moves (the function blowing up for r < 0, its derivative for 0 < r < 1, the logarithm's slow dive for the double root), and the Bessel functions of Figure 7.4 are built from their series with the order on a slider, J_p and J_{p+1} on the left settling term by term from x = 0 outward, Y_n or J_{−p} on the right according as p is an integer or not.
