## Exercises

End exercises (kind `exercise`), every one kept, answers checked by SymPy and numerics at build:
- 8.3.1 · a) (kπ, 0): k even J = [0 1; −1 −1], eigenvalues −½ ± i√3/2, spiral sinks, asymptotically stable; k odd J = [0 1; 1 −1], eigenvalues (−1 ± √5)/2, saddles. b) for μ² < 4g/L the same: even k spiral sinks (−μ/2 ± i√(4g/L − μ²)/2), odd k saddles (−μ/2 ± √(μ² + 4g/L)/2). c) a pendulum with a little friction swings with decreasing amplitude and settles hanging down; balanced upside down it still falls. `open`.
- 8.3.2 · a) γ = 0.001: (0, 0) saddle, (400, 0) saddle, (100, 30) spiral sink: the populations settle at 100 hares and 30 foxes. b) γ = 0.01: (0, 0) saddle, (40, 0) sink: the foxes die out and the hares settle at 40 (the third point (100, −60) is outside the quadrant). Phase diagrams described. `open`.
- 8.3.3 · a) the partials of xy e^(−x−y) vanish only at (1, 1), where the Hessian is negative definite; b) the same for y^a x^d e^(−cx−by) at (d/c, a/b) (the log of C is strictly concave on the quadrant). `open`.
- 8.3.4 · the time around is ∫₀^(2π) dθ / √((2g/L) cos θ + 2g/L + ω₀²). `open`.
- 8.3.5 · a) ω = ±√((2g/L)(cos θ − 1) + ω₀²) (C = ω₀² − 2g/L); b) ω₁ = 2√(g/L): below it the square root vanishes before θ = π and the pendulum turns back, above it ω never vanishes; c) at ω₁ exactly the pendulum approaches the top forever and never reaches it (the separatrix: infinite time, Figure 8.6's curve through the saddle). `open`.
