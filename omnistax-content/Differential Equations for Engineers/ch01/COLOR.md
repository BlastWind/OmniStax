# Chapter 1 colour plan

Prepared 2026-10-19 with `config.md`. It refines the book's `COLOR.md` for what this chapter draws; root rules 7 and 22 hold, and nothing here invents a hue. No fact and no convention: the book's plots are black on white.

## What the chapter colours

- `function`: the unknown y(x) or x(t) of a first-order equation and every solution of one, general, particular, implicit or singular, every solution curve, the family drawn thin beside the one picked out, the right-hand side f(x, y) or f(x) of y′ = f(x, y) (a function of x and y), the factors f(x) and g(y) of a separable equation, the coefficient p(x) and the right side f(x) of a linear equation, the integrating factor r(x) and u(x, y), the substitution v, the temperature T(t), the amount of salt x(t), the population x(t), the potential F(x, y) and its parts M, N, the PDE's unknown u(x, t) and its initial data f(x); an equilibrium solution x = A is a constant function drawn as a dashed level.
- `derivative`: y′, dy/dx, x′, dT/dt, v′, the partial derivatives F_x, F_y, M_y, N_x, u_x, u_t, the total derivative dF, du/ds along a characteristic, and every short segment of a slope field, drawn lighter than the curves so the solution reads first.
- `integral`: an antiderivative ∫f(x) dx, the definite integral ∫ from x₀ to x of f(t) dt of (1.2) and (1.4) and its shaded area, the exponent ∫p(x) dx of an integrating factor.
- `point`: an initial condition (x₀, y₀), the point a solution is drawn through and every draggable start, a critical point of an autonomous equation (stable filled, unstable hollow), the foot (ξ, 0) of a characteristic.
- `vector-field`: the arrows (M, N) of an exact equation in 1.8.
- `curve`: a level curve F(x, y) = C of an exact equation (an implicit solution drawn as a path, not a graph), a characteristic curve ξ = constant of 1.9.
- `error`: the error of Euler's method, |actual y − approximate y|, drawn as the gap between the polyline's end and the solution, and the error column of Tables 1.1 and 1.2.

Euler's polyline and the improved Euler and Runge–Kutta steps wear `function` drawn thin with dotted vertices (an approximate solution is a function of the same role), so the gap to the exact solution reads in `error`.

In ink: the independent variable x or t; arbitrary constants C, D, A(y)'s constant; k, A (the ambient temperature), M (a limiting population), h (harvesting), α, a, b, c, g of a PDE as parameters; the step size h and the index i; the slopes k₁…k₄ of Runge–Kutta written as numbers; the characteristic coordinates ξ and s as variables.
