## Why this chapter

Chapter 29 made light a stream of photons and matter a wave. This chapter turns
both ideas on the atom. It starts with the evidence that atoms exist at all
(Brownian motion), takes the atom apart (Thomson's electron, Millikan's charge,
Rutherford's nucleus), builds Bohr's hydrogen atom, uses it for x rays, lasers and
fluorescence, explains its quantization by the electron's wave, and ends with the
quantum numbers and the exclusion principle that give the periodic table its
shape. It is the book's bridge from physics to chemistry.

## BE INSPIRING (rule 23)

The atom cannot be seen, and this chapter is the story of how people learned what
is inside it anyway. The figures should let the reader do the experiments.

**Fire alphas at gold.** Rutherford's result is a surprise only if you expect
something else. Let the reader choose Thomson's plum pudding or a nucleus, send a
stream of alphas at the foil, and watch the counter: with the pudding nothing comes
back; with the nucleus, one in thousands bounces straight home. The reader has
disproved a model with their own tally.

**One hydrogen atom, three views.** Orbits growing as $n^2$, a level ladder
crowding toward zero, and a spectrum strip beneath, all live together. Click a
transition and a photon leaves in its true colour, its line lighting up on the
strip in the right series; the Balmer lines are the only ones you can see, and the
reader sees why. A $Z$ choice turns it into He⁺ or the tungsten $K_\alpha$ of 30.4.

**A wave that must close on itself.** Drag the radius of an orbit and the
electron's wave wraps around it, meeting itself crest to crest only at $n = 1, 2,
3$; between them it tears. Quantization stops being a rule and becomes a fit.

**Cones in space and a filling table.** Turn $L$ on its cones for $l = 1, 2, 3$;
then fill shells electron by electron, the exclusion principle refusing the third
electron in 1s, and watch the rows of the periodic table appear.
