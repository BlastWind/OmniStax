## Why this chapter

Chapter 25 built the thin lens and the mirror; this chapter puts them to work.
The eye is a single thin lens a fixed distance from a screen, and every defect
of vision and every pair of spectacles is a question about that lens's power.
The microscope and the telescope are two lenses in a row, the image of one being
the object of the next. Color vision leaves geometry altogether for the three
kinds of cone and the eye-brain system that compares them, and the chapter ends
on the ways real lenses fall short of the thin lens. It uses no new law: it is
the chapter in which the reader's optics becomes instruments they own and use.

## What is new

The eye as an optical system (the thin lens equation rewritten in terms of
power, with the image distance pinned to the retina), accommodation and its
loss, the near and far points and the two spectacle corrections, the addition
of powers of lenses in contact, color as a perception rather than a wavelength,
the multiple-element system with its product of magnifications, numerical
aperture and the $f$-number, angular magnification, and the aberrations. The
chapter introduces no new physical type; every length is a `position`.

## Locked views and 3D (root rule 28)

Every figure of the chapter is a planar ray diagram, a graph or a photograph,
and is flat (28.1). Two drawings the book prints in perspective argue a locked
view (28.2): the acceptance cone of Figure 26.17(b) and the fiber's acceptance
cone of 26.18, both cones about the optical axis that a flat section shows
fully; and Chandra's nested mirrors in 26.26(b), which stay a photograph-like
drawing and are kept as the book's image. No figure argues a full 3D scene: the
cones are symmetric about the axis and a section through it loses nothing.

## Motion

Optics has no clock. Accommodation (26.4) is a change the reader drives with the
object distance, not a history; nothing in the chapter registers a cycle, and
every figure answers its sliders.

## Rule 23: what is inspiring here

The reader leaves able to read their own prescription: a negative number means a
diverging lens and a far point that is not infinity, and one slider of object
distance against one slider of power shows why. The eye figure should let the
reader move an object in from infinity and watch the lens thicken to keep the
image on the retina, then switch the eye to myopic or hyperopic and add the
spectacle that puts the image back. The microscope and telescope figures should
make the chained image visible: drag the object and watch the first image slide
across the eyepiece's focal point, turning the final image from virtual to real.
