## Why this chapter

This is where the book goes inside the nucleus. Chapter 30 found the nucleus as a
tiny, heavy centre of the atom; this chapter asks what it is made of, why some
nuclei last forever while others fall apart, and why the energy they give off is
a million times what any chemical reaction gives. Its answers are few and strong:
nucleons packed like marbles ($r = r_0 A^{1/3}$), three kinds of decay each
obeying a short list of conservation laws, a clock that is purely statistical
($N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$), mass turned into energy ($E = (\Delta m)c^2$) and a
single curve, the binding energy per nucleon, that explains stability, the
energy of decay and, in the next chapter, fission and fusion. The chapter ends
on the book's first real quantum effect, tunneling.

## What is new

- The nucleus as a thing with parts: nucleons, $Z$, $N$, $A$, nuclide notation
  ${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X}_{N}$, isotopes, the unified atomic mass unit and MeV/$c^2$.
- Two new forces (strong and weak) and two new conservation laws (electron
  family number, nucleon number), plus the neutrino and antimatter.
- The first purely statistical law of the book: $N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$, the
  half-life and the decay constant, activity in Bq and Ci.
- $E = (\Delta m)c^2$ used as a working tool with atomic masses, and the binding
  energy per nucleon curve.
- Tunneling, the book's first quantum mechanical barrier penetration.
- Two new types: `activity` (decays per unit time, $R$) and `decay-constant`
  ($\lambda$). Both are drawn and coloured in 31.5; neither is a frequency (a
  decay constant is a probability per unit time and an activity is a count per
  unit time), and nothing is coerced into `frequency` to save a hue.

## BE INSPIRING (root rule 23)

The chapter's two great ideas are a statistical clock and a curve, and both can
be seen being made rather than stated. For the clock: a grid of a few hundred
nuclei, each one flipping a fair coin every half-life and going dark when it
loses, while the smooth $N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$ curve is drawn beside the jagged
count; the reader sees that no nucleus ages, that the half is only true of many,
and that a small sample wanders (31.5's conceptual question on "exactly half"
is answered by looking). Dating then becomes a slider on the fraction left
reading back an age, with the shroud's 92% as a detent. For the curve: the
binding energy per nucleon plotted over the chart of the nuclides, so that the
valley of stability, the iron peak and the ⁴He spike are one picture, and a
chosen decay (α, β⁻, β⁺) moves a nuclide one step on the $N$–$Z$ chart toward the
valley, with the conserved $A$ and charge tallied as it moves. The whole ²³⁸U
series can be walked this way, step by step, down to ²⁰⁶Pb. Tunneling, finally,
is best felt with an energy slider: raise the α's energy and the barrier thins,
the wave's tail outside grows, and the half-life readout falls by powers of ten,
which is Gamow's law in one gesture.
