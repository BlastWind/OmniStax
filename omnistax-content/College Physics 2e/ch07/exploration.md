# Exploration: College Physics 2e, Chapter 7 Work, Energy, and Energy Resources

Written before the chapter was prepared (2026-09-11). The source of record is
the CNXML bundle (`source/osbooks-college-physics-bundle`). The book's
organisation (book → chapters → sections → untitled narrative headers, one
CNXML module per section, all the apparatus inside the module) is as recorded
in `ch02/exploration.md`; nothing differs here.

## Why this chapter

Chen asked for the rest of the book in one job, and Chapter 7 is the first of
the second wave. It is where the book stops adding up forces and starts
adding up energy. Nine sections take the reader from the scientific
definition of work, through kinetic energy and the work-energy theorem,
gravitational and elastic potential energy, the split between conservative
and nonconservative forces, and the law of conservation of energy, to power,
the energy the human body spends, and the world's energy resources. Four
placeholder concepts have pointed at this chapter since Chapter 16 was
built: `work` (7.1), `kinetic-energy` (7.2), `potential-energy` (7.3) and
`conservation-of-energy` (7.6).

## Observations that affect the plan

- Energy is the new thing, and the chapter's drawing vocabulary is the
  energy account: a bar or a stack that shows how much of a total is
  kinetic, how much potential and how much has gone to friction, standing
  beside the scene that spends it. Nearly every figure from 7.2 on is a
  scene whose energy can be drawn that way, so a sim that runs a scene and
  keeps its energy account beside it covers many of the book's figures at
  once. The other recurring shape is the area under a force-distance graph,
  which is the work (7.2's Figure 7.3, 7.4's Figure 7.10(c), 7.1's crane
  item).
- One new type is wanted, and the case for it is that 7.7, 7.8 and 7.9 draw
  it. The book declares eleven types and none of them is an energy per unit
  time. `power` is defined in 7.7 as a quantity of its own with its own unit
  (the watt, and the horsepower beside it), it is the whole content of
  Table 7.3, Table 7.4 and Table 7.5, and 7.9's scatter of power per capita
  against GDP per capita plots it on an axis. Plan.md names `power` among
  the canonical types a chapter may declare, so the id is `power`, the label
  is "power" and the dimension is "W". Nothing else in the chapter earns
  one: work, kinetic energy, every potential energy, thermal energy, other
  energy and the food energy of 7.8 are all energies and take the energy hue
  the book already declares, told from each other by their subscripts as the
  book writes them, and efficiency is a ratio of two energies and stays in
  ink, as the coefficients of friction of 5.1 do. Mass, the angle between a
  force and a displacement, and the percentages of 7.9 stay untyped and in
  ink, as Plan.md decided.
- The symbol table needs thirty-one new rows: twenty-five energies, one
  power, three positions and one untyped ratio. The energies
  are the chapter's whole apparatus of bookkeeping and the sections colour
  them constantly: `W_net`, `W_nc`, `W_c`, `W_s`, `W_app`, `W_fr`, `W_gr`,
  `W_N`, `W_total`, `W_out`, `PE`, `PE_g`, `PE_s`, `KE_i`, `KE_f`, `PE_i`,
  `PE_f`, `ΔKE`, `ΔPE`, `ΔPE_g`, `OE`, `OE_i`, `OE_f`, `E` and `E_in`. The
  power is `P`. The positions are `d`, the magnitude of a displacement,
  which the chapter writes in every work equation, and `x_i`, `h_i` and
  `h_f` from the conservation equation of 7.4. The ratio is `Eff`, which
  gets a row with its LaTeX only. A thirty-second row, `v_i`, the initial
  speed that 7.4 and 7.5 write where Chapter 2 wrote $v_0$, was staged and
  then dropped: Chapter 8 or 9 merged it first, so the chapter uses theirs.
- One macro name had to be chosen round an existing row. `\kPE` is already
  the elastic potential energy of 16.1, so the chapter's plain `PE` takes
  `\kPEtot`, in the style of `\kFtot` and `\kvtot`, and `PE` without a
  subscript is indeed the total potential energy of a system, since the book
  says "there is a potential energy associated with each force, and the
  energy only changes form between KE and the various types of PE". The
  report asks for the 16.1 row to be renamed so that `\kPE` can come back to
  the plain symbol.
- 7.4's potential energy of a spring, ${\text{PE}}_{s}=\frac{1}{2}k{x}^{2}$,
  is the same physics as 16.1's `elastic-potential-energy`, which was built
  first because Chapter 16 was built first. A chapter may not restage
  another chapter's concept, so this chapter stages `elastic-potential-energy`
  at 7.4, which is where the book introduces it, and the report asks for the
  duplicate to be settled.
- The chapter leans on Chapter 4 for force, the net external force, weight
  and the second law, which the work-energy theorem is derived from; on
  Chapter 5 for kinetic friction and its magnitude, which 7.5's sliding
  examples use; on Chapter 2 for displacement, speed, the acceleration due
  to gravity and ${v}^{2}={{v}_{0}}^{2}+2ad$, which is the step that turns
  net work into kinetic energy; on Chapter 3 for the component of a force
  along a displacement, which is the $\cos\theta$ of the definition of work;
  and on 16.1 for Hooke's law and the force constant, which 7.4's spring
  needs. The book itself points at 5.3 for Hooke's law, at 2.7 for falling
  objects, at 6.5 for the universal law of gravity and at 4.3 for the
  second law, and all four of those chapters are built.
- Three PhET notes are dropped, as the built chapters drop them, and each
  section's `notes` says so: Energy Skate Park in 7.4, The Ramp in 7.5 and
  Masses and Springs in 7.6.
- 7.9's problem set is eleven items headed Integrated Concepts, Unreasonable
  Results, Construct Your Own Problem and Critical Thinking, and not one of
  them tests renewable resources or world energy use. They are the chapter's
  closing integrated set: each needs ideas from several sections, and the
  reader is ready for them only at the end, which is exactly what rule 12
  asks, so they stay in 7.9. The one exception is the Critical Thinking item
  (`exer-86622`, two boxes released by two pistons), which turns on the work
  done by a varying force as the area under a force-distance graph and on
  the work-energy theorem, both of them 7.2's; it is keyed, and it is held
  for 7.2.
- 7.9 states no equation and 7.8 states none outside its one worked example,
  so both sections carry no rows on the formula sheet. Neither is folded
  into a neighbour (rule 11).
- 7.6's narrative is one photograph and two tables, and 7.9's is three
  charts and a photograph. Both sections will want a sim of their own to
  have anything to hold: the energy account of a whole process for 7.6, and
  the book's own charts, transformed, for 7.9.
- The book's own numbered summary of steps, Problem-Solving Strategies for
  Energy, sits in 7.6 as a boxed note of six steps, and 7.4's and 7.7's
  problem sets each point a reader back at it. It is kept verbatim as a
  note, with the book's numbered steps.
- Two of 7.5's figures, 7.16 and 7.17, are the same baseball player sliding
  to a stop, once on the level and once on a 5° slope. They fold into one
  sim with the angle on a slider, which reports "Figure 7.16 + 7.17" and
  keeps both numbers. No other run of figures in the chapter is one scene
  drawn several times: 7.2's (a) to (e), 7.3's (a) and (b) and 7.5's (a) and
  (b) are sub-figures under one number, not folds.
- The CNXML types 7.9's Critical Thinking item `problem-exercises` where
  every other item of the chapter is `problems-exercises`, as 4.5's problems
  were. All of them are classed by the header they sit under, as this book's
  rules say.
- The converter writes a cross-module reference as `[m42151](module:m42151)`
  and loses what the link pointed at. The CNXML's own target ids say what
  the book prints, and a section that meets one writes the book's words:
  7.1's gasoline problem and 7.2's aircraft-carrier problem point at
  Table 7.1; 7.1's spring AP item points at Figure 7.10(c); 7.2's three
  references in its first paragraphs point at Figure 7.2; 7.4's pogo-stick
  problem and 7.7's car-on-a-slope problem point at the Problem-Solving
  Strategies for Energy note of 7.6; 7.8's *Daedalus 88* problem points at
  Table 7.2; and 7.9's swimmer problem points at the swimmer figure of 7.8's
  problem set and its fat-metabolising item at Table 7.5.
- The swimmer figure of 7.8's problem set (`Figure_08_08_06a-6b66.jpg`) is
  wanted by 7.9's third problem as well. It is copied once, in 7.8, as the
  paths figure of 3.2 was, and carried on both cards.
- No bundle file this chapter keeps has a space in its name, so every
  `originals` entry is the bundle's own file name.
