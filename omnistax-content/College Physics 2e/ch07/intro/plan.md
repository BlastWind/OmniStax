## What the page is

The chapter's own introduction, kept as a page of its own and listed before
7.1, as rule 21 asks. It is the book's four paragraphs on what energy is:
that it takes many forms, that the total is constant, that conservation of
energy rests on experiment and on the clay-bed measurements of Willem ’s
Gravesande and Émilie du Châtelet, that energy resources limit economic
growth, and that energy is loosely the ability to do work, which is why the
chapter begins with work. The book sets no learning objectives, no summary
and no exercises here, so the page carries none of them, and it has no lead,
since nothing is written in the book's place.

The four paragraphs fall into two passages and the page marks them as two:
`conservation`, which carries the photograph and the two paragraphs on
energy and its conservation, and `energy-and-work`, which carries the
societal paragraph and the working definition that leads into 7.1.

The two equations of the second paragraph, $E = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ and
$E = mc^2$, are set in plain LaTeX. The page draws nothing and colours
nothing, and by rule 7 a page colours only the types it binds, so no `\k`
macro is used here even though the chapter gives $E$ and $v$ their types.

## The marked terms

The book marks two terms in these paragraphs. `energy`, "the ability to do
work", is already a glossary row of 7.1, so rule 21 leaves it there and the
page adds nothing to the glossary. "Conservation of energy" is marked in the
second paragraph but the book gives it no `<definition>` of its own
anywhere; the nearest glossary row is 7.6's "law of conservation of energy",
which the chapter's tables carry under that section. The report says so.

## The photograph

Figure 7.1, the wind farm in Iowa at sunset, is kept with the book's number,
its caption and its credit clause. Rule 21 makes the chapter opener's
photograph the point of the page, and the caption is a question put to the
reader about the page's subject: how many forms of energy can you identify
in it. The CNXML gives the image neither a width nor a height, so `widths`
is empty and the app shows the image at its natural size.

The numbering is the book's: the opener is Figure 7.1, so 7.1's first figure
is Figure 7.2. The numbers were read off the publisher's own pages for all
nine sections and for this introduction, and they agree with counting every
figure of the narrative, the worked examples and the boxed notes in order
and passing over the figures that sit under Test Prep for AP Courses,
Conceptual Questions and Problem Exercises.
