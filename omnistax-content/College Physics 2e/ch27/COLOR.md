# Chapter 27 colour plan

Prepared 2026-09-28 in the chapter's prep pass, beside `config.md`, and brought
under root `RULES.md` item 7 on 2026-10-04. This chapter uses the book's
declared categories and the app's selected palette. It declares no category of
its own and writes no hue except where a hue is the colour of light itself.

Wave optics measures lengths against a wavelength, so most of what this chapter
colours is one category, `position`: the wavelength, the separation of two
slits, the width of one, the diameter of an aperture, the thickness of a film
and the distances to and along a screen. Every angle wears `angle`, as it does
from Chapter 3 on. The order $m$, every index of refraction and the numerical
aperture are counts or ratings and stay in ink.

A figure that shows both $\lambda$ and $d$ tells them apart by their brackets and labels, never by a second hue |

Of item 7's four ways this chapter uses three.

**Facts.** Light has the colour of its wavelength, as in Chapter 25: a 633-nm
He-Ne beam and its fringes are drawn red, a 450-nm beam blue, white light fans
into its true spectrum in a grating's higher orders, a thin film shows the
colour it reflects, an unlit screen is black and an LCD pixel is the grey of the
light it passes. Every such colour goes through `F.fact`, so the reader's Facts
and conventions switch reaches it; with facts off a pattern of light falls back
to ink at its brightness and its black ground to a soft panel. A wavelength
slider wears the `position` hue because its number is a length, while the light
it controls wears the colour that number means. Fact precedes referent, so a ray
of coloured light keeps its colour and the referent it belongs to is told by its
label.

**Referents.** Each section lists in `referents` the particular things its
figures draw and its text names, and the figure draws each with `F.ref`: the
vacuum and the medium (27.1); the ray, the old and new wavefronts, the mirror and
the two media, and the wall, door and listener of the doorway (27.2); the slits,
the screen, the two waves and their resultant, and the two paths (27.3); the
screen, the double slit and the grating (27.4); the slit (27.5); the two objects,
the antenna, the objective, the specimen, point P, the two point objects, the
lens and the focal spot (27.6); ray 1, ray 2 and the film, and the two slides and
the spacer (27.7); the rope and the slit, the two filters, the surface and its
three rays, the filter face on with its molecules and electron, the sunlight and
the molecule that scatters it, and the polarizer, layer, analyzer and pixel
(27.8); the object and the background (27.9). Where two waves of one colour of
light must be told apart, one is dashed and the label carries the referent hue.

**Categories** from the scheme, as the table gives them. A category is coloured
on every page where a symbol of it is written or a phrase names a particular one.

A medium is never tinted to say what it is; where a medium is a referent its
region takes a faint wash of its referent hue and its label the full hue. The
element palette arises once, for the electron of 27.8, which keeps `F.el('e-')`.
The introduction has photographs only and colours nothing.
