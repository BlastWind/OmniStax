## Why this chapter

Chapter 28 is the first chapter of modern physics. It starts from two postulates,
that the laws of physics are the same in every inertial frame and that the speed
of light is the same for every observer, and follows them wherever they lead:
simultaneity is not absolute, moving clocks run slow by the factor $\gamma$,
moving lengths contract by the same factor, velocities no longer add like
numbers, light from a receding source is red-shifted, and momentum and energy
must be redefined with $\gamma$ in them. The last section turns $E = \gamma mc^2$
into rest energy, mass-energy conversion and the speed limit $c$.

## What is new

The first chapter where the reader's intuition is wrong by design; every section
answers "which observer measures what". New quantities: $\gamma$ (untyped),
proper time $\Delta t_0$, proper and contracted length $L_0$ and $L$, the second
velocity $u'$, source and observed wavelengths, rest energy $E_0$, relativistic
and classical kinetic energy. No new type is needed: they are times, positions,
velocities, frequencies, momenta and energies. Six Check Your Understanding
boxes, the first since Chapter 23, each needing a `data-place` host.

## The chapter's answer to root rule 23: be inspiring

Relativity is the one subject where a still picture cannot show the claim,
because the claim is that two pictures of the same event disagree. The chapter's
figures should show both frames of one event side by side on one clock: the light
clock ticking straight up and down in the ship while the same light zig-zags in
the Earth's frame, with the two tick counts diverging by $\gamma$ as the reader
drags $v$ toward $c$; the muon that cannot reach the ground in the classical
count and does in both relativistic ones, from the Earth by living longer and
from the muon by a shorter atmosphere; a canister whose $u'$ slider pushes toward
$c$ while its Earth-frame velocity bends away from the classical line and never
crosses $c$. One $\gamma$ curve, drawn once in 28.2 with dashed specials at the
book's named values (3.20 at $0.950c$, 30.0 for the twin), should recur as the
shape behind 28.19 and 28.22, so that the reader sees the one wall at $c$ reappear
as infinite time, infinite momentum and infinite energy. The Doppler figure can
paint the observed line in its true colour as the source recedes, the chapter's
one physical-fact colour.
