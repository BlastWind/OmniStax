## Why this chapter

Chapter 31 built the nucleus and its decays; this chapter uses them. Its first
half follows radiation into the body: a tagged compound carries a nuclide where
the body sends it, a camera outside reads its γ rays, and the damage the same rays
do to DNA is measured as energy per kilogram, weighted by how concentrated the
ionization is. Its second half follows the binding energy per nucleon curve to
its two ends: light nuclei fuse, heavy nuclei split, and in both a little mass
becomes a great deal of energy, in the Sun, in a reactor and in a bomb.

## What is new

- Radiation dose as energy per kilogram, the rad and gray, the RBE, and the rem
  and sievert; low, moderate and high doses; the linear hypothesis.
- Medical imaging with nuclei: tagging, the Anger camera, SPECT and PET.
- Radiotherapy and the therapeutic ratio; food irradiation.
- Fusion and fission read off the BE/A curve; the Coulomb barrier and tunneling in
  the Sun; the proton-proton cycle; chain reactions, critical mass and breeding.
- One new type, `dose` (Gy): drawn in 32.2 to 32.4 and coloured by their
  readouts; it is not `energy`, since it is divided by the mass of tissue.

## BE INSPIRING (root rule 23)

Three pictures carry the chapter. First, the PET ring: annihilations flicker
inside a head, each drawing a faint chord between two detectors, and the chords
pile up until the bright spot appears where no single chord could place it; a
toggle to SPECT's collimated single photons shows why PET's image is sharper.
Second, dose as a ratio: the same joule dropped into a whole body and into a
forearm, then the same dose given by γ rays (sparse ionization along a long
track) and by α particles (a dense short track), with the RBE weighting the
sievert readout, and the Table 32.4 bands lit as the dose climbs. Third, the
binding energy curve as a map with two roads: drag two light nuclei up the left
slope and they fuse, drag a heavy one down the right and it splits, with the
energy released read off the height gained; the Coulomb barrier then shows why
the left road needs ten million degrees and tunneling. For fission, a chain
reaction whose neutrons per fission, $x$, is a slider: below one it dies, at one
it holds steady like a reactor, above one it runs away like a bomb.
