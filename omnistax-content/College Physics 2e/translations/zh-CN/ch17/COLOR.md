# Chapter 17 colour plan

Prepared 2026-09-14 and brought under item 7 on 2026-10-04. The rule is root
`RULES.md` item 7 and its four ways; this file records what is particular to
the chapter. Every category is coloured on every page, in the prose where a
phrase names a particular one, in the maths and in the figures. No figure
hard-codes a hue.

## Categories

| Quantity | Type | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity $I$, the threshold $I_0$, two compared intensities $I_1$ and $I_2$ | `intensity` | The bar that grows as a pressure amplitude is raised, the intensity axis of the decibel ladder, the reflected and transmitted shares at a boundary, and every readout that writes $I = P/A$, $I = (\Delta p)^2/2\rho v_\text{w}$ or $I/I_0$. The reflected and the transmitted intensity of 17.7 are one type, told apart by direction and label |
| Frequency $f$, the source and observed frequencies $f_\text{s}$ and $f_\text{obs}$, the harmonics $f_n$, $f_1$, $f_2$, $f_3$, $f'$, the beat frequency $f_\text{B}$ | `frequency` | The slider that sets a source going, the fork's label, every Doppler, harmonic and beat readout, and the frequency axis of the hearing graphs of 17.6 |
| Speed of sound $v_\text{w}$, the source and observer speeds $v_s$ and $v_\text{obs}$, the blood speed $v_\text{b}$, the rms speed $v_\text{rms}$, $v$ in $Z = \rho v$, $c$ | `velocity` | The arrow on a moving source or observer, the speed a wavefront grows at, and the readouts that write $v_\text{w} = f\lambda$ or a Doppler ratio |
| Wavelength $\lambda$, $\lambda'$; the length $L$ of a tube; a depth $d$ to an echo's reflector; the lever arms $r_1$, $r_2$ of the middle ear; an air displacement | `position` | The bracket between two compressions, the tube's length and the bracket under it, the standing wave's displacement curve, the echo's depth and the lever arms |
| Pressure amplitude $\Delta p$, the gauge pressure on a graph's axis, $P_1$ and $P_2$ at the eardrum and the oval window; the forces $F$, $F_1$, $F_2$ | `pressure`, `force` | Every gauge-pressure axis and amplitude bracket, the arrows on the eardrum and at the oval window. A compression is told by packing, never by a tint |
| The areas $A$, $A_1$, $A_2$ of the eardrum, the oval window and a surface the power crosses | `area` | The area sliders of 17.1 and 17.6 and the readouts that write $F = PA$ or $P_2 = F_2/A_2$ |
| Acoustic impedance $Z$, $Z_1$, $Z_2$ | `acoustic-impedance` | The impedance written under each medium in 17.7's boundary and the readout of $a$ |
| Temperature $T$, density $\rho$, power $P$, time $t$, mass $m$, the cone angle $\theta$ | `temperature`, `density`, `power`, `time`, `mass`, `angle` | Coloured wherever they are written; the cone's angle and its arc in 17.4 wear the angle hue |
| Sound intensity level $\beta$ in dB, loudness in phons, the intensity reflection coefficient $a$, the harmonic number $n$, the Boltzmann constant $k$, the Mach number | Untyped | Ink, including the sliders that set them. The decibel axis of the ladder is ink beside an intensity axis in its hue |

The level is not the intensity. $\beta$ is a logarithm of a ratio of
intensities and the book calls it unitless; every readout that writes
$\beta = 10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$ colours $I$ and $I_0$ and leaves $\beta$ and the
"dB" in ink, and a slider on $\beta$ is an ink slider that moves a coloured
bar.

Air is dots, and a wavefront is where they are dense. No page tints a
compression darker or a rarefaction lighter; the packing of ink dots is the
pressure, and a gauge-pressure graph beneath draws the same thing as a curve
on a pressure-hued axis. A wavefront drawn as an arc is an ink arc. The air
itself is the medium of the whole chapter and is not a referent.

## Referents

Each section lists the things its figures draw and its text names, and the
figure draws each with `F.ref`: the vibrating string and the eardrum of 17.1;
the tuning fork, the bat and its insect, the woofer and the tweeter of 17.2
(whose $f_1$ and $f_2$ wear their colours in the subscript); the loudspeaker,
the listener and the ring of sources of 17.3; the car and observers X and Y,
the supersonic source, and the aircraft over its observers of 17.4; the
engine noise and the sound the headphones introduce (one curve each, their
sum in the pressure hue), the fork and its tube, and the tubes of the ladder
and of Example 17.5 in 17.5; the sound marked on the equal-loudness grid, the
two ears of the audiogram, and the eardrum, hammer, anvil, stirrup and oval
window of the middle ear in 17.6, where the subscripts 1 and 2 of $P$, $F$
and $A$ name the eardrum and the oval window and those of $r$ the hammer and
the stirrup; the two media at a boundary ($Z_1$, $Z_2$), the transducers and
probe, and the artery and its blood ($v_\text{blood}$) in 17.7.

## Facts and conventions

No page draws a fact colour or a named element. A section agent who draws a
named gas (the helium of 17.5's voice problem) draws its atoms in `F.el('He')`.
Turning colour off leaves the labels, the packing of the dots and the arrow
directions sufficient to read every figure.
