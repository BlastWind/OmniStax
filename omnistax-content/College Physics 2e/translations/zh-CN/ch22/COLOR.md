# Chapter 22 colour plan

Prepared 2026-09-15 and settled by the chapter pass on the same day; brought
under root `RULES.md` item 7 on 2026-10-04. Colour reaches these pages in item
7's four ways, fact, convention, referent and category, and where two apply the
earlier wins. This file records what is particular to the chapter; it invents
no hue.

These stay in ink: the permeability of free space $\mu_0$, a constant of
nature; the number of turns $N$ and the turns per unit length $n$, counts; the
number of carriers $n$ per unit volume in 22.7.

A word or phrase wears a category when it names a particular one the reader can
point at: the field of 0.50 G the glass rod crosses, the current in the
head's winding, the Curie temperature of iron. The law, its definition and the
kind in general stay ink.

## Referents

Every page names particular things that its figures draw, and each is a
referent of its section, drawn with `F.ref` and marked `<span data-ref>` in the
text: the hanging and the buried magnets of 22.1, the iron and the original
magnets of 22.2, the compasses, mark, loop and wire of 22.3, the glass rod of
Example 22.1, the oxygen ions, Van Allen belts and tokamak chamber of 22.5, the
conductors, vessel and magnet of 22.6, the wires, ducts and magnets of 22.7, the
motor's loop, magnet, shaft and brushes and the meter's coil, pole faces, spring
and needle of 22.8, the wire, solenoid and right hand of 22.9, wires 1 and 2,
the arc and the current balance of 22.10, and the spectrometer, tube, patient
and field sources of 22.11.

A referent's body, outline and name wear its colour; a quantity drawn on it
keeps its category's, so the arrow along a wire is current and the wire is the
referent. A wire seen end-on is a ring in the wire's colour with the current's
dot or cross inside it. Where a scale shows one point per referent, as the field
strengths of 22.4 and 22.11 do, each point takes its referent's colour.

Two figures of one section may draw the same thing. The glass rod of 22.4, the
solenoid of 22.9 and the MRI magnet of 22.11 each keep one row, with the figure
whose referent order leaves no clash, and the other figure calls the same
`F.ref` so that the thing wears one colour on the page.

Where a symbol's subscript names a referent the subscript wears its colour:
$I_1$, $F_1$ and $B_1$ wear wire 1's in 22.10 and $I_2$ and $F_2$ wire 2's; $m_1$
and $m_2$ in 22.5's readout wear the two oxygen ions'.

## Facts and conventions

A particle the page names is drawn from the element palette: the electron,
proton and neutron of 22.2's atomic models, the cosmic-ray and tokamak protons
of 22.5, the electron of 22.11's cathode ray tube, the protons of its MRI slice
and the oxygen of its velocity selector. Two isotopes of one element share an
element colour, so the two oxygen ions of 22.5 and of 22.11's spectrometer are
referents instead. A carrier the page does not name, 22.6's Hall carrier, is
drawn in `charge` with its sign lettered on it, since naming it would answer the
section's question in advance. The one colour that is the physical fact is the
introduction's aurora photograph, kept as the book prints it.

## The sense of a field line round a current

A magnet has an N and an S to say which way its lines run, and its closed lines
carry no arrowhead. A current has no poles, so every closed field line drawn
round a current carries its sense on the page: an arrowhead on the line itself,
as 22.10 puts one at the top of each circle; a field arrow tangent to the line,
as 22.9 draws at three points of its circle; or a compass standing on the line,
as 22.3 does. With colour off a field line is still told from a force arrow by
its shape, and every readout stays legible from its symbols.
