# Chapter 25 colour plan

Prepared 2026-09-15 in the chapter's prep pass, beside `config.md`, and brought
under root `RULES.md` item 7 on 2026-10-04. This chapter uses the book's declared
categories and the app's selected palette, as items 7 and 22 require. It declares
no category of its own and hard-codes no hue except where a hue is a physical fact.

Geometric optics is geometry: a ray diagram is made of straight lines,
perpendiculars, angles and surfaces. What it colours by category is the angles
those lines make and the few quantities that carry a dimension.

| Quantity | Category | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| The speed of light $c$ in a vacuum, and the speed $v$ of light in a material | `velocity` (Chapter 2) | One hue; the slowed speed in a medium keeps it, as it already does in 24.2, and the two are told apart by their symbols and by the medium they are drawn in |
| The focal length $f$, the object and image distances $d_{\text{o}}$ and $d_{\text{i}}$, the object and image heights $h_{\text{o}}$ and $h_{\text{i}}$, the radius of curvature $R$ of a mirror, the wavelength $\lambda$, and the lengths of 25.7's solar trough | `position` (Chapter 2) | One hue for every measured distance along or across the axis, as a wavelength has worn it since 16.9; a negative distance is told by its sign in the readout and by which side of the lens it is drawn on, never by a second hue |
| Every angle: $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$, $\theta_{\text{i}}$, $\theta_{\text{r}}$, $\theta_{\text{c}}$, the apex of the prism, the rainbow's 42° and the sun's elevation | `angle` (Chapter 3) | The arcs, their names, the angle sliders and the readouts |
| The round-trip time of Michelson's measurement, and the period of his rotating mirror | `time` (Chapter 2) | On 25.3's moving figure and in the text that names them |
| The area, heat, temperature rise, density, volume and power of 25.7's solar trough | `area`, `energy`, `temperature`, `density`, `volume`, `power` | In the worked example's text and maths |

The index of refraction $n$, the magnification $m$ and the power $P$ of a lens or
mirror in diopters are ratings or wait for a place, as the book's `COLOR.md` says,
and stay in ink, with the frame, the surfaces' hatching, the normals and every ray
that carries no colour of its own.

Of item 7's four ways this chapter uses three.

**A colour that is the physical fact.** The colour of light is the chapter's
subject from 25.5 onward, and it is drawn as the fact, not as a category hue. A
ray's colour is its wavelength: a 660 nm ray is drawn red because it is red, a
410 nm ray violet because it is violet, and the band of Figure 25.21, the fan
leaving the prism of Figure 25.22, the light leaving the drop of Figure 25.23 and
the arc of Figure 25.24 are all painted in true spectral colour, with sunlight its
pale yellow. Every one goes through `F.fact`, and they are the only literal colours
in the chapter's figures. A coloured ray is never a categorised thing: the hue says
which wavelength the ray is, and the wavelength slider beside it wears the
`position` hue because the number is a length, while the ray it controls wears the
colour that number means.

**Referents.** Each section's `referents` names the particular things its figures
draw and its text points at, and the figure draws them with `F.ref`: the Sun, Earth,
window, car and person of 25.1; the flashlight, surface and observers, and the
person, her image and the mirror of 25.2; the fish, tank and eye, Michelson's two
mirrors, source and observer, and the two media of 25.3; the two media, the fiber,
its cladding and its neighbour, the two ends of the bundle, the two prisms and the
cut gem of 25.4; the prism, the drop and the rainbow's observer of 25.5; the lenses,
the numbered rays, the person and her image, and the film, retina and two lenses of
25.6; and the bottle, its image, the mirrors, the eye, the object and its image and
the three numbered rays of 25.7. Medium 1 and medium 2 of 25.3 and 25.4 split the
subscripts of $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$. Instances the text never names one by one
(the two paths of the fish tank, the two rays of Figure 25.32) keep `F.cat`, offset
past the figure's referents.

**Categories.** The category hues carry the angles, speeds, distances and times
above.

The element palette does not arise in this chapter: nothing in it is an atom, an
ion or a molecule.

A medium is never tinted to say what it is. Water, glass, diamond and air are told
by their labels, by their index written beside them and by the way the ray bends at
the boundary, not by a wash of colour over the region, exactly as a phase and a
temperature are told by shape and packing elsewhere in this book. The one tint any
figure may lay over a region is a faint neutral panel that separates two media, and
it carries no meaning beyond the boundary it makes visible; where a medium is a
referent, its name wears its referent colour.

With colour off every figure stays legible from its labels, its ray directions and
its caption alone.
