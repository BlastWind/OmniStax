# Chapter 15 colour plan

Prepared 2026-09-28 with `config.md` and brought under root `RULES.md` item 7
on 2026-10-04, when only the introduction was built. Colour reaches these pages
in item 7's four ways, fact, convention, referent and category, and where two
apply the earlier wins. This file records what is particular to the chapter;
it invents no hue.

## Categories

| Quantity | Type | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| An ion concentration, a molar solubility, [Ag⁺], [NH₃], a complex's concentration, pH, pOH | `concentration` | Every log concentration axis wears it |
| K_sp, Q_sp, K_f, K_d, the net K of coupled equilibria, K_a2 | `equilibrium-constant` | The K_sp line on a concentration plane is in this hue, its axes in `concentration`; Q_sp is its variant away from equilibrium |
| Grams dissolved, a gram solubility | `mass` | 15.1, where a readout states it |
| A volume of solution mixed | `volume` | Where a precipitation example mixes two |

These stay in ink: x, the exponents p and q, the logarithm terms, the
stoichiometric coefficients.

A word or phrase wears a category when it names a particular one the reader can
point at: the molar solubility of CuBr, 7.9 × 10⁻⁵ M, the K_sp of AgI in its
Check Your Learning, the pH at which Mg(OH)₂ begins to precipitate. The kind in
general ("the solubility of a substance can vary…", "the solubility product"
as a definition), its unit and its definition stay ink.

## Facts and conventions

`F.el` for every ion and molecule (Ag⁺, Cl⁻, Ca²⁺, F⁻, water, NH₃, the complex
ions), the ion keeping its element colour and a charge mark. Through
`F.fact`: a precipitate's own colour (AgCl white, AgBr cream, PbCrO₄ yellow,
Ag₂CrO₄ red), unchanged in both themes. The donated electron pair of a Lewis
equation is an ink arrow.

## Referents

Each section lists its own when it is built. Planned:

- **15.1.** The salt and its two ions in a dissolution figure; the two salts of
  a selective precipitation (AgCl and AgBr, or AgCl and Ag₂CrO₄), one line or
  region each on the concentration plane; the solution with and without a
  common ion, where the common-ion examples compare them.
- **15.2.** The metal ion, the ligand and the complex of a formation
  equilibrium.
- **15.3.** Al³⁺ and Al(OH)₄⁻ on one solubility-against-pH graph, one curve
  each; the two coupled reactions of a net equilibrium, their K's split, the
  subscript in each reaction's colour.

The frame of a figure and its labels stay in ink, and with colour coding off
every figure stays legible from its labels.
