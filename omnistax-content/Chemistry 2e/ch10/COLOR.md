## Temperature, pressure and energy

The melting and boiling points the text quotes for a particular substance, the
temperatures and pressures of a particular state or example, and the energies
of a particular transition are marked; a boiling point or a vapor pressure
named as a kind ("the normal boiling point of a liquid is defined as…") stays
ink. 10.3 writes q, m, c, ΔT and n of the heating curve with their macros
(`\kq`, `\km`, `\kcspec`, `\kdT`, `\kn`) and the condensation, freezing and
deposition enthalpies as `\kdH` with their subscripts, each with a variables
row in the section.

## Lengths, angles, areas and the properties of a liquid

- **10.2.** The capillary equation is written with its macros: `\khcap`,
  `\kTsurf`, `\kthetacont`, `\krtube`, `\krho`, `\kgrav`. In Figure 10.20 the
  heights, the diameters and the 2r of the close-up wear length, the contact
  angle wears angle, and the readout writes each number in its category.
- **10.1.** The contact band of the pentanes is an area, as the text's
  "surface area available for contact" says.
- **10.6.** The edge length (`\kacell`, and `\kledge` where Example 10.14
  writes l), the face diagonal (`\kddiag`), the radius (`\kratom`), the plane
  spacing (`\kdplane`) and the Bragg angle (`\kthetabragg`) are on their
  macros. The general cell's a, b, c, α, β, γ have no symbol rows and stay as
  the book prints them; the arcs of Figure 10.55 + 10.56 wear angle. The order
  n of the Bragg equation is a count and stays ink.

## Atoms, ions, forces, phases

- **Atoms and ions** take the element palette through `F.el` in every figure.
  The atoms of 10.53 + 10.54 are identical spheres with no element, so the
  layer positions A, B and C, which the text names one by one, are referents.
- **Partial charges.** The text marks the partial charges it names on HCl as
  charge. The δ+ and δ− ends of the dispersion and dipole figures keep the pair
  `F.cat(0)` and `F.cat(1)`, named in a legend: they recur across both figures
  and the text names the ends of a kind, not one particular end.
- **Intermolecular attractions** (dotted lines, hydrogen bonds) are ink.
- **Phases** are told by packing, never by colour; the regions of a phase
  diagram are named in ink.
- **Physical colours**: iodine's purple vapor (10.27), the wine in 10.19, and a
  substance's own colour in a photograph stay as fact.

## What stays in ink

The order n, the coordination number, counts of atoms per cell, the percent of
space filled, radius ratios, electronegativities, viscosity (no category of
the book), the region names on phase diagrams, and every axis rule and label
that is not a quantity of a category.
