## Why this chapter

Chapter 20 is the book's short tour of organic chemistry, built on the bonding of Chapters 7 and 8. 20.1 explains carbon's variety of compounds, then walks the hydrocarbons: alkanes with their sp³ zigzag chains, condensed and skeletal structures, the smooth rise of melting and boiling points with chain length (Table 20.1), structural isomers, IUPAC naming of alkanes, substituents and alkyl groups, combustion and fractional distillation, substitution reactions and functional groups; alkenes, their geometric isomers and addition reactions; alkynes; benzene and the aromatic hydrocarbons; with a Recycling Plastics box introducing polymers. 20.2 adds oxygen by single bonds: alcohols (fermentation, hydration of ethylene, naming) and ethers (naming, dehydration of alcohols, MTBE), with a box on carbohydrates and diabetes. 20.3 turns to the carbonyl group: aldehydes and ketones, oxidation and reduction of carbon read as C–H bonds traded for C–O bonds, alcohols oxidized to aldehydes, ketones and acids, then carboxylic acids, esters, fats and oils. 20.4 adds nitrogen: amines as weak bases, heterocyclic pyridine, amides and amidation, with boxes on DNA, alkaloids, proteins and enzymes, and Kevlar, closing on an unnumbered table of every functional group in the chapter.

## Root rule 28

Physical 3D: the molecules as ball-and-stick and space-filling models (20.2, 20.4, 20.7, 20.10, the butane isomers), turned about a single bond or held rigid about a double one; benzene's ring with its p orbitals is mathematical 3D if drawn. A locked view argues for nothing here.

## BE INSPIRING

Grow an alkane one carbon at a time and watch its boiling point climb past room temperature until the gas becomes a liquid and then a wax, the chain zigzagging longer in 3D as it goes. Twist n-butane about its middle bond and see the book's three different-looking drawings turn out to be one molecule, then try the same twist on 2-butene and feel the double bond refuse, cis and trans held apart. Send crude oil's vapours up a fractionating tower and watch each fraction condense on the tray where it cools below its boiling point. Trade methane's C–H bonds one by one for C–O bonds and watch carbon's oxidation number climb from −4 to +4 through methanol, formaldehyde and formic acid to carbon dioxide. Link two amino acids and see the water leave and the peptide bond form, the first step of every protein.
