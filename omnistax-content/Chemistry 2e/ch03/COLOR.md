Five of the book's categories. A category is coloured on every page, so the prose marks a particular one wherever the reader can point at it; a mention of the kind in general stays ink.

## What stays in ink

A count of atoms or molecules and Avogadro's number (a count per mole), the subscripts of a formula and the formula units per molecule (`n_fu`, never the amount hue), a percent, ppm and ppb, a mass concentration in mg/dL, a mole ratio and the "divide by the smallest" step, the flowchart boxes and arrows. A mass percentage is a ratio of two masses and is not a mass.

## Facts, conventions and referents

Every atom and molecule, the models of chloroform, aspirin, NaCl, ibuprofen, glycine, dopamine and saccharin and any solute particle, is drawn through `F.el`; the sodium purple and chlorine green of the book's NaCl model are the element palette, and the element shares of the 3.2 composition bar are tinted with it. The colour of a solution is a physical fact drawn through `F.fact`: the blue of copper nitrate in 3.16, paling as it is diluted (`CU_NITRATE`), and the water of the molarity beaker (`WATER`). It is never the concentration hue, which is on the slider, the symbol and the readout, not tinting the liquid.

The referents are the containers and samples the text names; particles keep their element colours.

The photographs (the eight element samples of Figure 3.5, the four compounds of 3.6) are kept as printed and carry no referents.

Nothing is coerced: molar mass is a mass (per mole), not an amount; molarity is a concentration, not an amount; a percent is not a mass.
