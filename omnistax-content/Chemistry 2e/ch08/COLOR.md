## Energy

Every energy of the chapter is one category: the curve, axis and readout of
Figure 8.2 with the bond energy at its minimum, the E axis of every orbital
energy-level and MO diagram, and the band gap bracket of Figure 8.39. The
orbital lines and the electron half-arrows on them are ink; an orbital line
stands for an orbital, and its height on the axis is what carries the energy.
The text marks the energies of the H₂ pair in 8.1 and the bond energies the
paragraphs quote, the energy of the H₂ molecule in 8.4 and the band gap where
it names the gap of the solid the figure shows.

## Lengths, angles and mass

- **Mass.** The weight of the sample that the Gouy paragraph of 8.4 compares
  in and out of the field, and its increase. A balance reads a mass, and the
  book's caption of Figure 8.27 says mass.
- **Length.** The internuclear distance of Figure 8.2, on its axis and its
  slider, and in the text the bond distance of 74 pm and the distance between
  the nuclei the paragraph follows.
- **Angle.** The arcs and the degrees written on them in every hybrid figure
  of 8.2, the angle of the water Sim and its readout, the rotation of a CH₃
  group in Figure 8.17, the twist of a CH₂ group in Figures 8.23 + 8.24 + 8.25
  and the phase difference of Figure 8.28, each on its slider and in its
  readout. The text marks the bond angles it quotes for a particular molecule
  (water's 104.5°, ammonia's 107.3°, H₂S's 92.1°, H₂Te's 90°) and the angles of
  the hybrid sets the figures draw; an ideal angle stated of every tetrahedron
  stays ink.

## Orbitals, phases, atoms

- **Atoms** take the element palette through `F.el` in every figure. A nucleus
  drawn as the book's dot is ink.
- **Orbital lobes are not a category.** A lobe is a surface of a wave function,
  and its sign is the one thing about it the reader must tell apart. The two
  phases take `F.cat(0)` and `F.cat(1)`, the same pair in every figure of the
  chapter; the legend or the caption names them as the two phases of the wave
  function. In the mixing figures of 8.2 the book's "s blue, p red, hybrid
  yellow" is `F.cat(0..2)`, the same three in every figure, named in a legend
  or a hover name. These are kinds of orbital that recur across the chapter's
  figures, not a particular thing of one figure, so they keep one colour each
  rather than a referent hue that would change from figure to figure.
- **Nodes** are drawn as a dashed plane or line in ink.
- **Electrons** in energy-level and MO diagrams are ink half-arrows; unpaired
  ones are set apart by position, never by a hue.
- **ψ and Ψ** are the wave function, a category of the book: 8.2 writes ψ as
  `\kpsi` and 8.4 writes Ψ as `\kPsi`, each with its variables row.

## Referents

- **8.4, Figure 8.27.** The Gouy balance (beam and stand), the sample in its
  tube, the two electromagnets and the weights on their dish, drawn with
  `F.ref` and marked in the caption and in the paragraph that compares the
  weights. The sample's contents keep their element colour.
- **8.4, Figure 8.28.** The two waves and the wave of their sum, one curve and
  one label each.
- **8.4, Figure 8.39.** The valence band and the conduction band, which the
  note on band theory names one by one; the band gap between them keeps the
  energy hue.

Particles keep their element colours whatever the text calls them: an atom, an
ion or a molecule is never a referent. Lewis structures are notation and stay
ink.

## What stays in ink

Bond order, the counts of σ and π bonds, of electrons and of unpaired
electrons, the N of band theory, the Lewis structures, the letters of the
hybridization names, and every axis rule, arrow and label that is not a
quantity of a category.
