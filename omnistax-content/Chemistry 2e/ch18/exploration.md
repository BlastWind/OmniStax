# Exploration: Chemistry 2e, Chapter 18 Representative Metals, Metalloids, and Nonmetals

Written 2026-10-05, before the chapter was prepared. The thirteen modules were converted with `python3 tools/convert.py 18` and read in full. ## Why this chapter

The book's descriptive chapter. Chapters 2, 6 and 7 built the periodic table, its trends and its bonds; Chapters 13 to 17 built equilibrium, acids and bases and electrochemistry. Chapter 18 walks the representative elements group by group and spends those tools on real substances. 18.1 sorts the representative elements into metals, metalloids and nonmetals and reads the representative metals by group (1, 2, 12, 13, 14, 15): reactivity from ionization energy, the inert pair effect, passivation, allotropes. 18.2 isolates those metals from their ores by electrolysis (Downs cell, Hall–Héroult cell, molten MgCl₂) or by chemical reduction (Pidgeon process, carbon reduction of zinc and tin). 18.3 treats the metalloids, boron and silicon in detail: covalent crystals, Lewis acidity, zone refining, borates, silica against carbon dioxide, silicates. 18.4 gives the nonmetals' rules (electronegativity, the first member of each group, five redox patterns, acid anhydrides, disproportionation) and the allotropes of carbon, phosphorus and sulfur. 18.5 to 18.12 then take hydrogen, the carbonates, nitrogen, phosphorus, oxygen (oxides, peroxides, hydroxides, nonmetal oxides and oxyacids), sulfur, the halogens and the noble gases in turn.

## BE INSPIRING

Make the periodic table a map the reader can walk with their hands on the substances. Pick a group and watch the trend the text states play out as real things: lithium floating on oil, sodium's yellow flame, magnesium burning in CO₂, zinc fizzing in acid while mercury sits unmoved. Turn the crystals: silicon's tetrahedral network beside the puckered sheets of arsenic and the spiral of tellurium, then roll a sheet of graphene into a nanotube and close C₆₀. Heat sulfur and see the crowns open, the chains lengthen and tangle, the liquid darken and thicken until it will not pour at 230 °C, then thin again. Chill brown NO₂ and see it pair into colourless N₂O₄. Let electricity pull sodium out of molten salt and the halogens push one another out of solution in the order of their potentials, and end on xenon, the "noble" gas that Bartlett made react.
