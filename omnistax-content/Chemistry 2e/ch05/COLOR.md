## Variants

Heat, work, internal energy and enthalpy are one type and share the energy hue (the book's `COLOR.md`); q and w are told apart by label and by arrow style, heat as a wavy or plain arrow and work as a piston push, never by hue. ΔH°, ΔH<sub>f</sub>° and ΔH<sub>c</sub>° are told by their marks. T<sub>initial</sub> is hollow or dashed, T<sub>final</sub> filled, ΔT a bracket between them in the same hue. Heat gained and heat lost differ by the arrow's direction and sign, not by colour.

## What stays in ink

Specific heat c and heat capacity C (material properties chosen or computed, as density is in Chapter 1), the stoichiometric coefficient n, a percent, Calories per gram, the apparatus outlines, labels and axes. A metal or a solution in a calorimeter is not tinted by its temperature (root rule 7); the particles of 5.1's hot and cold water are water molecules in the element palette, oxygen red and hydrogen white through `F.el`, their speed the only sign of temperature, where the book draws them red and blue as a tint. A formula in a thermochemical equation is ink; any molecule drawn is in element colours. The flame of the torch and a food label keep their physical colours in photographs. Nothing is coerced into a neighbouring type to save a colour.
