# Chapter 14 colour plan

Prepared 2026-09-28 with `config.md` and brought under root `RULES.md` item 7
on 2026-10-04, when only the introduction was built. Colour reaches these pages
in item 7's four ways, fact, convention, referent and category, and where two

it invents no hue.

## Categories

| Quantity | Type | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| [H₃O⁺], [OH⁻], [HA], [A⁻], [B], [HB⁺], pH, pOH | `concentration` | pH and pOH are its logarithmic variants, so every pH axis wears it |
| K_w, K_a, K_b, K_a1 to K_a3, K_b1, K_b2, pK_w, pK_a, pK_b | `equilibrium-constant` | A conjugate-pair ladder (14.7 + 14.8) is a K ladder in this hue |
| T | `temperature` | 14.1 and 14.2, where K_w is read at 25 °C against 80 °C |
| Moles of strong acid or base added, moles of titrant | `amount` | 14.6's slider, Table 14.2 |
| The titrant volume | `volume` | 14.7's axis and slider |

These stay in ink: the percent ionization, x, the logarithm terms, the
stoichiometric coefficients.

A word or phrase wears a category when it names a particular one the reader can
point at: the 25.00 mL of 0.100 M HCl titrated in the strong acid–strong base example, the pH of a
particular solution in a worked example, the 25 °C at which two water molecules in
a billion are ionized. The kind in general ("the pH of a buffer" in an
objective, "the ion-product constant for water" as a definition), its unit and
its definition stay ink.

## Facts and conventions

`F.el` for every molecule and ion: H₂O, H₃O⁺, OH⁻, HF, F⁻, NH₃, NH₄⁺,
pyridine, the acid and base of 14.3's Sim, the aquo aluminium ion of 14.13, the
oxyacids of 14.12; an ion keeps its element colour and carries its charge as a
mark. Through `F.fact`: an indicator's acid and base forms (methyl orange,
bromthymol blue, phenolphthalein, universal indicator) wherever a flask, beaker
or interval band is tinted. The book's red-to-blue arrow on Figure 14.2 is
decoration: a redraw uses universal indicator's real colours as fact or stays
ink.

## Referents

Each section lists its own when it is built. Planned:

- **14.1.** The two conjugate pairs of an acid-base equation, the acid with its
  conjugate base and the base with its conjugate acid, as the book colours each
  pair; a phrase naming one pair lists both members.
- **14.3.** The acid and its conjugate base of the Sim and of each ICE table;
  the strong and the weak acid where the text compares them; K_a and K_b of a
  conjugate pair split, the a and b in the acid's and the base's colours.
- **14.5.** The stepwise species of a diprotic acid (H₂A, HA⁻, A²⁻), K_a1 and
  K_a2 split, the 1 and 2 in the steps' colours.
- **14.6.** The buffer and the unbuffered water of the buffer example, one pH curve
  each; acetic acid and acetate (14.15).
- **14.7.** The strong-acid and weak-acid titration curves of 14.18, one curve
  each; the indicators of 14.20 are facts.
