Consider an N-dimensional Hilbert space spanned by an orthonormal basis, $\{|e_1\rangle \ldots |e_N\rangle\}$ whose evolution is governed by a time-dependent Hamiltonian, $\kH(\kt)$. At any given time $\kt = \kt_0$, we can always find the instantaneous eigenstates and eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian $\kH(\kt_0)$,

$$\kH(\kt_0)|\epsilon_k(\kt_0)\rangle = \keps_k(\kt_0)|\epsilon_k(\kt_0)\rangle. \tag{7.1}$$

An arbitrary state may be expanded in the basis of instantaneous eigenstates,

$$|\kpsi(\kt)\rangle = \sum_l \kcinst_l(\kt)|\epsilon_l(\kt)\rangle, \tag{7.2}$$

and it obeys the Schrödinger equation $i\hbar|\dot{\kpsi}(\kt)\rangle = \kH(\kt)|\kpsi(\kt)\rangle$. For a constant Hamiltonian, a system prepared in an eigenstate will remain in that state; this is in general no longer true for time-dependent Hamiltonians.

Expanding the Schrödinger equation, we find that the time dependence of the eigenstates introduces new terms.

This set of N coupled differential equations may also be represented in matrix notation, with

$$\kpsi(\kt) = \begin{pmatrix} \kcinst_1(\kt) \\ \kcinst_2(\kt) \\ \vdots \\ \kcinst_N(\kt) \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{and} \quad \kH(\kt) = \begin{pmatrix} \keps_1(\kt) & & & 0 \\ & \keps_2(\kt) & & \\ & & \ddots & \\ 0 & & & \keps_N(\kt) \end{pmatrix}, \tag{7.5}$$

whereby Eq. (7.4) becomes

$$i\hbar\frac{d}{d\kt}\begin{pmatrix} \kcinst_1 \\ \kcinst_2 \\ \vdots \\ \kcinst_N \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \keps_1 & -i\hbar\langle\epsilon_1|\dot{\epsilon}_2\rangle & \ldots & \\ -i\hbar\langle\epsilon_2|\dot{\epsilon}_1\rangle & \keps_2 & & \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \\ & & & \keps_N \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \kcinst_1 \\ \kcinst_2 \\ \vdots \\ \kcinst_N \end{pmatrix}, \tag{7.6}$$

where the terms are implicitly time dependent for notational simplicity.

7.1.1 The condition for adiabaticity

Suppose the quantum state $|\kpsi\rangle$ is initially prepared at time $\kt = 0$ in one of the instantaneous eigenstates $|\epsilon_k(0)\rangle$. The off-diagonal couplings in Eqs. (7.4,7.6) indicate that $|\epsilon_k(\kt)\rangle$ is not an exact stationary state, but our intuition tells us that it should be approximately stationary for a Hamiltonian which changes very slowly. The situation where $|\epsilon_k(\kt)\rangle$ is essentially stationary is known as the “adiabatic” limit, and corresponds to off-diagonal terms sufficiently small that negligible population leaks to other states, i.e. $|\kcinst_k|^2 \approx 1$.

The requirement that $|\epsilon_k(\kt)\rangle$ be stationary (or $|\kpsi^{(1)}\rangle = 0$) thus provides our adiabaticity condition.

A Hamiltonian which changes on a timescale $\ktau$ will lead to couplings $|\langle\epsilon_l|\dot{\epsilon}_k\rangle| \propto 1/\ktau$, so the system does indeed adiabatically follow the instantaneous eigenstates for a slowly varying Hamiltonian.

Note that the adiabatic theorem is formulated for coherent Hamiltonian evolution of the system. For systems which exhibit decay, the adiabatic requirement may be modified from the form exhibited in Eq. (7.10). Even when the adiabaticity condition is satisfied, the neglected diagonal terms may still introduce a phase factor known as a “geometric” or “Berry's” phase. This geometric phase adds to the so-called “dynamical” phase (given by $e^{\{-i/\hbar\int_0^{\kt} d\tau\,\keps_k(\tau)\}}$) which comes from the diagonal part of the Schrodinger equation. A detailed discussion of such phases can be found in many modern quantum mechanics books (e.g. Sakurai); here we only note that in situations where the dynamical phase vanishes, there is frequently also no geometric phase.
