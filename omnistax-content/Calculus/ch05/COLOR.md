# Chapter 5 colour plan

Prepared 2026-10-12 with `config.md`. It refines the book's `COLOR.md` for what this chapter draws; root rules 7 and 22 hold, and nothing here invents a hue. The types are the 18 of `book.json`.

## What the chapter colours

No fact and no convention: the opener, the skydivers, the wingsuit and the iceboat are photographs in their own colours. The categories:

- `integral`: every Riemann sum and its rectangles (L_n, R_n, upper and lower sums), the area A under a curve and the shaded region that is it, A₁ and A₂, the definite and the indefinite integral and the words that name them, an antiderivative F, the accumulated area F(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t) dt drawn as a graph of its own, displacement and total distance, net change.
- `function`: f, g, the integrand and its curve, a velocity v(t) where it is integrated, the price–demand p(x), Q(t) and G(t), f_ave as a height of f.
- `derivative`: F′ and g′ in the Fundamental Theorem, the marginal price–demand p′(x), and the rates of change the net change theorem integrates where the text names them as rates.
- `set`: a partition P and its subintervals, an interval written in interval notation.
- `point`: c of the Mean Value Theorem for Integrals as the point (c, f(c)), x-intercepts that split a total area.
- `line`: a vertical asymptote (5.6, x = 10).
- `limit`: L only where it is a limit; the word in "limits of integration" names the bounds, which are numbers and stay ink.

In ink: x, t, u and the variable of integration; a, b, c as numbers; n, i, k and the index of a sum; Δx; the constants C, m, M and the parameters of the applications; the sample points xᵢ*.

## Rules the sections keep

- A curve wears `function`; the region under it and the rectangles that approximate it wear `integral`, the rectangles drawn over a lighter fill of the region so the gap between the sum and the area reads.
- A rectangle or region below the axis is the same `integral` hue, drawn with a dashed edge, since it counts against the sum: a variant by decoration, never a second hue.
- In the Fundamental Theorem the accumulated area and the graph of F are one colour, `integral`, and the slope of F at x, which equals f(x), is drawn as a short tangent in `line` with its slope written in `derivative`.
- f_ave is a dashed level in `function`; the rectangle of equal area under it wears `integral`.
- Two curves of one graph (f and g of Example 5.13) are referents, each in its referent colour, and the gap between them wears `integral`.
