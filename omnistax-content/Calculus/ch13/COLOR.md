# Chapter 13 colour plan

Prepared 2026-10-13 with `config.md`. It refines the book's `COLOR.md` for what this chapter draws; root rules 7 and 22 hold, and nothing here invents a hue. The types are the 18 of `book.json`.

## What the chapter colours

No convention. Facts: the photographs (13.1, 13.22) in their own colours. The categories:

- `curve`, the chapter's main type: a vector-valued function r(t), u(t) and the position s(t) of a projectile, with the plane or space curve each traces; a helix; a reparameterization and an arc-length parameterization r(s); the osculating circle, drawn dashed as a variant of the curve it hugs. A position r(t) drawn as an arrow from the origin in standard position wears the curve's hue, since it is the function's value; the curve's own symbol and its path are one colour.
- `vector`: the derivative r′(t) and r″(t), a tangent vector, the unit tangent T, the principal unit normal N, the binormal B, T′, the velocity v(t), the acceleration a(t), the initial velocity v₀, a gravitational force F, i, j and k. The vectors of one frame wear `vector` all, told apart by their names (12's rule); a component a_T T or a_N N laid along T or N wears it dashed.
- `limit`: the limit L of a vector-valued function, and the point it marks.
- `integral`: the indefinite and definite integral of a vector-valued function, arc length s, the arc-length function s(t).
- `derivative`: the speed v(t) = ds/dt and the curvature κ, a rate of turning per unit of arc length.
- `plane`: the normal plane and the osculating plane, translucent.
- `function`: the component functions f, g, h, their derivatives f′, g′, h′ in `derivative`.
- `point`: a point r(t₀) of a curve marked as a dot, the centre of an osculating circle, the Sun at a focus.
- `angle`: θ, a launch angle or the polar angle of an orbit.

In ink: t, u and the endpoints a, b; a constant vector C (of integration, or r × v in the Kepler proof) and D, as constants are; the radius of curvature R = 1/κ; the scalar components a_T and a_N (12.3's rule for scalar projections); a range R, a height H, g, G, masses, periods and distances in A.U.

## Rules the sections keep

- A curve and the arrow r(t) from the origin to its point share the curve's hue; r′(t) and T(t) drawn at the point wear `vector`, so the reader tells position from derivative at a glance.
- The osculating circle is the curve's hue dashed; its centre a `point`; its radius a plain ink segment.
- No referents: the chapter names no two curves of one figure apart (Example 13.6's r and u are worked symbolically, never drawn).
