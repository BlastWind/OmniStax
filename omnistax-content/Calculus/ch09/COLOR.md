# Chapter 9 colour plan

Prepared 2026-10-12 with `config.md`. It refines the book's `COLOR.md` for what this chapter draws; root rules 7 and 22 hold, and nothing here invents a hue. The types are the 18 of `book.json`.

## What the chapter colours

No fact and no convention: the opener and 9.1's sunflower and Parthenon are images in their own colours. The categories:

- `sequence`: a sequence {aₙ} and each of its terms aₙ, bₙ, cₙ, the Fibonacci numbers Fₙ, a recursively defined xₙ; drawn as dots at (n, aₙ), never joined by a curve. A sequence's terms laid as segments or bars (the telescoping segments, the rectangles of the integral test read as terms) keep `sequence` where the drawing is of the terms.
- `series`: an infinite series Σaₙ, a partial sum Sₖ and the sequence of partial sums {Sₖ}, drawn as dots at (k, Sₖ) or as the zig-zag of an alternating series on a number line.
- `limit`: the limit L of a sequence, the sum S of a convergent series, the bounds A and B of the limit laws, and ρ of the ratio and root tests; drawn as a dashed level.
- `error`: the remainder Rₙ = S − Sₙ, drawn as the gap between a partial sum and the sum, and its bounds where the figure draws them as a gap.
- `function`: f with f(n) = aₙ, the function of the integral test, f(x) = 1/xᵖ, and the continuous f of 9.1's theorem.
- `integral`: the improper integrals of the integral test and of its remainder estimate, drawn as the area under f.

In ink: n, k, N and every index; ε, M, the ratio r and the initial term a of a geometric series, the power p of a p-series, the constants c and the parameters of the applications.

## Rules the sections keep

- A sequence and its series side by side: the terms below in `sequence`, the partial sums above in `series`, on one n-axis, the sum a dashed `limit` level through the partial sums.
- A sequence is dots only; a curve through them would read as a function. Where f with f(n) = aₙ is drawn with them, the curve wears `function` and the dots sit on it.
- Two series the text names apart (9.4) are referents: each partial-sum sequence takes its referent colour, and the terms of each the same.
