## What the chapter colours

No fact and no convention: Figures 3.1, 3.2, 3.10 and 3.20 are photographs in their own colours. The categories:

- `derivative`, the chapter's main type: f′(a) and the derivative function f′, f″ and the higher derivatives, dy/dx and the other Leibniz forms, m_tan and the difference quotient Q, the graph of f′, and every rate the book reads as a derivative: instantaneous and average rate of change, instantaneous velocity and velocity v, speed, acceleration a, the population growth rate P′, the marginal cost, revenue and profit MC, MR, MP, the rate of change of temperature T′ and of profit P′.
- `function`: every function the text names (f, g, h and k where they are functions, j, p, q, the composite h(x) = f(g(x)), the inverse f⁻¹, E(x) = eˣ, B(x) = bˣ, L(x) = ln x, an implicitly defined y), every curve that is the graph of one, and the applied quantities that are functions: position s(t), temperature T(t), cost C(x), revenue R(x), profit P(x), population P(t) and A(t), the price p(x).
- `line`: a secant line and a tangent line (`secant`, `tangent-line`), and the normal of an exercise.
- `limit`: the limits of the difference quotient where the text names them as limits (3.1, 3.2), one-sided limits at a corner (3.2).
- `set`: the interval I of 3.1's definitions and the open set S of 3.2.
- `angle`: θ of 3.7's right triangle and of the inverse trigonometric functions' values.

In ink: x, t, u, a (the number a function is evaluated at), the increment h, Δx and Δy, a coordinate; n, k, m, r as integers, constants or exponents; c and the constants b, c of Example 3.14; e and π; the theorems and rules as concepts (the product rule, the chain rule are untyped concepts: their spans are marked and stay ink).

## Rules the sections keep

- A curve of f wears `function`; the curve of f′ beside or below it wears `derivative`; the tangent line wears `line` solid, a secant `line` dashed [10, 10] (a variant by decoration, root rule 7.4), so the secant arriving at the tangent changes decoration, not hue. Readouts write the slope in `derivative`.
- A one-sided slope is a `derivative` with its side marked (the secant from the left dashed, from the right dotted, or an arrowhead), never a second hue.
- A point (a, f(a)) is drawn in `function`, the colour of the curve it sits on; a dot on the graph of f′ in `derivative`.
- An applied quantity wears its mathematical type: s(t), C(x), P(t) `function`; v(t), MC(x), P′(t) `derivative`. A speed |v(t)| is `derivative`.
- A function written only as a value ("f(3) = 9") is ink; the f in it wears `function`, the f′ in "f′(3) = 6" wears `derivative`.
