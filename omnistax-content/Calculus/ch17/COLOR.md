# Chapter 17 colour plan

Prepared 2026-10-13 with `config.md`. It refines the book's `COLOR.md` for what this chapter draws; root rules 7 and 22 hold, and nothing here invents a hue. The types are the 18 of `book.json`.

## What the chapter colours

No convention. Facts: the photographs (17.1, 17.5, 17.9, 17.12) in their own colours. The categories:

- `function`: the solution y of a second-order equation and its pieces y₁, y₂ and y_p, the right-hand side r(x), the functions u and v of variation of parameters, a displacement x(t) and its particular solution x_p, the charge q(t) on a capacitor, an external force f(t), a voltage E(t); a solution curve on every graph, the transient and steady-state parts of a forced motion.
- `derivative`: y′ and y″, y₁′, y₂′, y_p′, y_p″, u′ and v′, a velocity x′, an acceleration x″, a current I = dq/dt; a slope or a tangent's slope read off a solution curve (the tangent line itself wears `line`).
- `series`: the Bessel series and its partial sums.
- `point`: an initial point (0, y(0)), a boundary point, the moving point of a solution graph.

In ink: x and t as variables, the arbitrary constants c₁, c₂, the coefficients a, b, c, the roots λ, λ₁, λ₂, α, β of the characteristic equation and the plane they are drawn in, the physical parameters m, b, k, g, s, L, R, C, ω, A, ϕ, T, the frequency f, the series coefficients aₙ and Cramer's z₁, z₂. A spring, a mass, a dashpot, a wheel and a circuit are drawn in ink, their names as labels.

## Rules the sections keep

- A solution curve wears `function`; where a figure draws the complementary part and the particular part of one solution, both are `function` told apart by their names and by decoration (the particular solution dashed), never by a second hue.
- The roots of the characteristic equation are numbers and stay ink on their plane; the case they fall in is said in words, not in colour.
- A velocity or a current read off a curve is a `derivative`; its tangent line wears `line`.
- The physical scene is ink; the displacement x drawn on it as a bracket or a pointer wears `function`, so the scene and its graph read as one quantity.
